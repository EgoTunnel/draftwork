Using Draftwork with a class

Students write in Draftwork and upload one file to your course site. You open the files to see how each piece was written, and ask about a few moments in a short conversation. Draftwork never gives a score.

Before the assignment

  1. Open Draftwork, go to Assignments, and create the assignment (title, due date, and the link to the course dropbox if you have it).
  2. When asked, choose a folder for automatic backups, such as one in your college OneDrive. Your assignments and notes are kept there.
  3. Click Instructions for students, copy the text, and paste it into the assignment in your course site.
  4. In the course site’s dropbox settings, allow .draftwork files (or all file types).

After students upload

  1. In your course site, download all submissions as one zip file.
  2. In Draftwork, open Submissions and drop the zip on the page. Nothing is uploaded: the files open in your browser.
  3. Each class has a summary bar: red (a major flag, or several), amber (minor flags), green (no flags). Sort by Most serious first and work down the list.
  4. If a student uploaded a Word document instead, Draftwork lists them at the top so you can follow up.

Reading a submission

  • The sentence at the top says what to do: nothing, a quick question, or a short conversation.
  • Flags are reasons to ask, not conclusions. Each lists innocent explanations, and the student’s own note if they wrote one.
  • Questions to ask point to specific moments. Click one to see that moment in the replay.
  • File integrity only tells you the file wasn’t altered. It doesn’t show who wrote it.

The check-in

  • Ask two or three of the suggested questions, openly and neutrally. Someone who wrote the piece answers in seconds.
  • Record each answer and outcome in Draftwork. Print summary gives you a one-page record.
  • A flag is never evidence on its own. Follow your institution’s academic integrity process for anything further.
